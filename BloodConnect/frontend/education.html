KNOW YOUR BLOOD

Eight major blood groups.
One important difference.

Blood groups are determined primarily by the ABO and Rh systems. Knowing your blood group helps healthcare professionals determine compatible blood for transfusion.

Often called the universal red-cell donor, although actual transfusion decisions are made by healthcare professionals based on compatibility testing and clinical need.

03

BEFORE YOU DONATE

Can you donate today?

Eligibility is assessed by the blood donation centre. These are general factors, not a personal medical assessment.

Age & weight

You must meet the applicable minimum age and weight requirements.

CHECK
Current health

You generally need to be well and free from conditions that temporarily prevent donation.

CHECK
Haemoglobin

Your haemoglobin level may be checked as part of the screening process.

SCREENING
Recent events

Recent illness, procedures, travel, tattoos, medications or other factors may affect eligibility.

REVIEW

Final decision

Only the authorised blood donation centre can determine whether you are eligible to donate at a particular time.

Requirements can vary according to local regulations and individual circumstances.
04

PREPARE • DONATE • RECOVER

Make the experience
easier on yourself.

BEFORE

Prepare your body.

  • Eat a normal meal.
  • Drink adequate fluids.
  • Get adequate rest.
  • Answer health questions honestly.
DURING

Let the professionals guide you.

  • Follow the staff's instructions.
  • Tell staff if you feel unwell.
  • Remain relaxed during collection.
AFTER

Give yourself time to recover.

  • Rest after donation.
  • Have refreshments.
  • Drink plenty of fluids.
  • Avoid strenuous activity for the remainder of the day.
05

MYTHS VS FACTS

Let's separate
fact from fiction.

Blood donation is surrounded by common misconceptions. Here are some important distinctions.

01 “Donating blood will make me permanently weak.”
FACT

Some donors may temporarily feel tired or light-headed. The body replaces the donated blood components over time.

02 “I should donate on an empty stomach.”
FACT

Donors are generally advised to eat normally and maintain adequate hydration before donating.

03 “I can donate whenever I want.”
FACT

Donation frequency and eligibility depend on applicable guidelines and your individual circumstances.

04 “The blood donation centre does not screen donors.”
FACT

Donors undergo health screening before donation to determine whether they can safely donate at that time.
